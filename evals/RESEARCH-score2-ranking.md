# Rescuing RepoRadar's Score-2 Band: Synthesis Report

## 1. Problem statement

RepoRadar's pointwise Haiku gate has collapsed to a near-binary score distribution: almost everything admitted receives score 2, score 3 is rare (33 admits, 0 threes in the wild `gate_full_pool` sample), and within the score-2 band true precision spans 0.00 to 1.00 by repo. Under net@2, each shown paper contributes an expected 3p−2 where p is its true actionability probability, so the breakeven is p = 2/3 — yet the gate cannot distinguish 'diffusion' (10 score-2 admits, all actionable, net +10) from 'vectordb' (8 score-2 admits, p = 0.50, net −5), and the share of score-3 admits predicts band precision at only r = +0.30. The heuristic lexical ranker is flat within the band, so on an unranked band there is no interior optimum in digest size: show-all yields +1.91 mean net@2 (22 cases) and the score-3-only policy yields +0.50 with 16/22 abstentions, discarding +10/+4/+3 cases. What is needed is, ideally, a calibrated per-paper P(actionable) with a single global show threshold at 2/3 (handles both the all-good and all-bad tails), or failing that a reliable within-band ordering — with the hard constraint of zero per-repo tuned knobs, on an Anthropic-primary stack (no logprobs; OpenAI key available for logprobs; CPU-only local compute), at up to a few dollars per 22-repo benchmark run.

## 2. Approach families surveyed

### 2.1 Listwise permutation ranking (RankGPT / LRL)

**How it works.** Put the repo profile plus the entire candidate list in one prompt; the LLM emits a permutation of candidate IDs in preference order.

**Citations.** RankGPT arXiv:2304.09542; LRL arXiv:2305.02156; list-length effects arXiv:2412.14574.

**Measured numbers.** RankGPT: DL19 nDCG@10 75.59 (GPT-4) vs 50.58 pointwise relevance-generation; at nDCG@1 (top-of-list discrimination) 82.17 vs 54.26. LRL, the cleanest same-model comparison, at exactly our list size (10 candidates/prompt): 0.6580 vs 0.5975 pointwise on DL19 (~+6 points). Boundary condition: zero-shot listwise degrades beyond ~20-25 items (arXiv:2412.14574 studies ranking past the sliding-window regime) and is highly sensitive to input order — the direction is corroborated by permutation self-consistency work (arXiv:2310.07712), but the sweep's specific figures (nDCG@1 72.87 with a good initial order → 20.93 random → 3.88 reversed) did not verify against the cited source; treat the exact numbers as unconfirmed.

**Fit.** Our bands (10-25 papers) fit in one window, one call per repo, cost negligible. The 15-30-point listwise-over-pointwise gap is the strongest published evidence that the headroom is in comparative judgment. But permutation output is structurally incapable of abstention — it must rank an all-bad band — and all numbers are topical relevance, not utility. Since our band arrives effectively unordered (the regime where single-pass listwise is least reliable), any listwise method must be shuffle-aggregated. Ordering only; no calibration.

### 2.2 Subset selection with shuffle-vote aggregation (TourRank / Setwise / listwise-set / SETR)

**How it works.** Instead of a full permutation, each call is a selection: "select the papers a maintainer should act on — possibly none." Repeat R times over random shuffles of paper order; each paper's selection share in [0,1] is a Monte-Carlo pseudo-probability.

**Citations.** TourRank arXiv:2406.11678; Setwise arXiv:2310.09497; listwise-set / utility judgments arXiv:2403.19216; SETR arXiv:2507.06838; permutation self-consistency arXiv:2310.07712.

**Measured numbers.** TourRank-10 DL19 71.63 vs RankGPT 68.19 vs pointwise 62.51, and shuffling the initial order has "almost no effect" on TourRank while RankGPT drops substantially. Shuffle-aggregation improves listwise quality by 7-18% (GPT-3.5/4) to 34-52% (Mistral-7B). Most importantly, arXiv:2403.19216 is the only paper squarely on our task type — utility judgment over already-topically-relevant candidates — and its findings map one-to-one onto our failure: pointwise utility judgment collapses (F1 22.4 in the pointwise configuration; pointwise = high recall / low precision, i.e., "everything gets a 2"), listwise-set selection reaches F1 64.14, utility-explicit instructions beat relevance instructions 64.14 vs 48.97, and 10-shuffle voting adds +2.84% F1. Small open models were not good enough (Vicuna-13B 45.95).

**Fit.** Best structural fit found in any sweep. At n = 10-25 TourRank's multi-stage tournament degenerates to a single selection call repeated R = 10-15 times with shuffled order (~$0.5 Haiku / ~$3-6 Sonnet per benchmark run). Selection share gives ordering, a global threshold, and — uniquely among listwise-family methods — a native empty-set path for all-bad bands. **Caveat (load-bearing):** selection share is peer-relative consistency, not calibration; a uniform-garbage band can still produce high-share papers unless "possibly none" genuinely fires, and LLMs have output-size priors (tend toward ~top-half selection). This is the single behavior the experiment must verify on the 'linter'/'compiler' bands. No published controlled comparison isolates selection-vs-permutation at matched cost; evidence is convergent but not definitive.

### 2.3 Pairwise comparison with Bradley-Terry aggregation (+ anchoring for an absolute scale)

**How it works.** "Which of these two papers should this repo's maintainers act on first?" over all pairs, both presentation orders (inconsistent pairs = ties); fit Bradley-Terry to win results; rank by BT strength. Anchored variant adds 2-4 template-generated reference "papers" (e.g., a canonical borderline case: topically adjacent survey, no implementable technique) to pin the shift-invariant BT scale to an absolute zero point.

**Citations.** PRP arXiv:2306.17563; PairS arXiv:2403.16950; non-transitivity arXiv:2502.14074; Product-of-Experts soft comparisons arXiv:2405.05894; anchored BT (JP-TL-Bench) arXiv:2601.00223; negative result on post-hoc Elo calibration arXiv:2606.13221.

**Measured numbers.** PRP-Allpair DL19 72.42 vs pointwise 64.61 (+8); >10% avg nDCG@10 over pointwise on BEIR. PairS: Spearman with humans 0.44→0.56 (GPT-3.5) and 0.55→0.64 (GPT-4), with the largest gains for weak-to-mid judges — our Haiku regime. Ratings and rankings from the *same* annotator disagree ~60% of the time (arXiv:2308.15812), which is why near-binary absolute scores can hide a 0.00-1.00 precision spread. Position bias is severe and worst for Claude-family judges: MT-Bench swap-consistency GPT-4 65.0%, GPT-3.5 46.2%, Claude-v1 23.8%; order manipulation alone flipped 66/80 verdicts (arXiv:2305.17926). Round-robin+BT is measurably more robust than sorting/knockout under LLM non-transitivity; at n ≤ 25 full round-robin is 45-300 pairs — affordable, no Swiss-tournament economies needed. The conformal-Elo paper explicitly shows post-hoc calibration of pairwise outputs yields *relative* probabilities only — anchoring, not temperature fitting, is the label-free path to an absolute threshold.

**Fit.** Best-evidenced ordering mechanism (largest measured deltas in the closest-matched judge regime); ~1,200-2,500 Haiku calls ≈ $1-3/run with caching. Both-order querying is mandatory, not optional, on Claude. Pure pairwise delivers only need (a); anchoring is the only found route to (b) from this family, but it is the riskiest, least-validated component: anchor writing IS the calibration, synthetic abstracts may be stylistically distinguishable from real ones, and no paper validates anchored BT as calibrated in the proper-scoring-rule sense. Treat anchored-BT output as a monotone score with a principled zero, not a probability.

### 2.4 Distributional / fine-scale pointwise re-scoring (G-Eval, TrustJudge, scale granularity, Rank1 mechanism)

**How it works.** Attack the quantization directly: score on a finer anchored scale (0-10) and read the *distribution* over score tokens (OpenAI logprobs) or estimate it by N samples at temperature 1 (Anthropic), outputting the expectation. The Rank1 variant: reason first, then emit true/false; P = p("true" token).

**Citations.** G-Eval arXiv:2303.16634; TrustJudge arXiv:2509.21117; fine-grained labels arXiv:2310.14122; scale granularity arXiv:2505.19334; Rank1 arXiv:2502.18418; sampling stability arXiv:2510.27106.

**Measured numbers.** G-Eval named our exact pathology ("one digit usually dominates the distribution") and its expectation fix gains modestly (Spearman 0.500→0.514). TrustJudge's fine-scale + expectation cuts score-comparison inconsistency 23.32%→14.89% and pairwise-transitivity inconsistency 15.22%→4.40%. The best direct evidence on scale size (arXiv:2505.19334, GPT-4o): pointwise ranking quality rises roughly monotonically from 2-point to 11-point scales, and at 11 points pointwise becomes statistically indistinguishable from listwise on most dataset-model pairs — i.e., much of the listwise advantage may just be the coarse scale. Plateau (occasionally dip) beyond ~11 labels; raw 0-100 single-shot is noisy (round-number clustering). Rank1 nearly doubles monoT5-3B on BRIGHT (27.5-29.4 vs 16.8), the closest public analog to reasoning-intensive actionability; running it locally is out (CPU-only), but its scoring mechanism is replicable for pennies via OpenAI logprobs. Temperature-0 scoring measurably *degrades* judge quality (Rating Roulette) — variance is signal to harvest, not noise to remove.

**Fit.** Cheapest credible fix (~450 calls, <$1/run via gpt-4o-mini logprobs; ~$1-2 as N=10 Haiku samples). Directly targets the score-2 pile-up with zero architecture change. Produces a continuous score (ordering) and a defensible pseudo-probability (p(score ≥ cut) or p(true)) — but RLHF logprobs are overconfident, so a global monotone recalibration (LORO cross-fitted) may be needed before the 2/3 threshold is meaningful. One validity landmine specific to us: our judge is GPT-5.5, so an OpenAI-family ranker may score well against the labels partly via shared family bias — winners must be cross-checked against the 200-row Sonnet second-judge file.

### 2.5 Ensemble confidence: vote fractions, verbalized probability, panels, cascades

**How it works.** P(actionable) = agreement statistic over N re-judgments with varied few-shot/rubric contexts (Simulated Annotators), optionally pooled across model families (PoLL) and combined with verbalized probabilities elicited *after* forcing the model to state the best reason the paper is NOT actionable (the "consider the alternative" format). Near-threshold papers can escalate to a stronger judge.

**Citations.** Trust-or-Escalate arXiv:2407.18370; PoLL arXiv:2404.18796; Just Ask for Calibration arXiv:2305.14975; Xiong arXiv:2306.13063; self-consistency audit arXiv:2607.08065; CheckEval arXiv:2403.18771; FrugalGPT arXiv:2305.05176; dual-LLM systematic-review screening (medRxiv 2024.10.01.24314702).

**Measured numbers.** Simulated Annotators is the strongest published calibration construction: ECE 0.058-0.095 vs 0.217-0.374 single-shot across GPT-4-turbo/GPT-3.5/Mistral; failure-prediction AUROC 0.723 vs 0.642. PoLL: panel of small cross-family judges beats a single GPT-4 judge (kappa 0.763 vs 0.627 on NQ) at 7-8x lower cost. Verbalized probabilities beat token probabilities for RLHF models (~50% relative ECE reduction; Claude 2 ECE 0.049 in the top-k format) but cluster at multiples of 5 in the 80-100 range and discriminate weakly alone (failure AUROC 0.52-0.63) — they must be averaged over samples (Avg-Conf), not used single-shot. The sober counterweight: agreement is only a weak-positive correctness signal (Spearman 0.20-0.59), 48% error persists even at agreement ≥ 0.8, and confident-wrong consensus recurs *across* OpenAI and Claude families — the exact mechanism by which 'vectordb' impersonates 'diffusion'. Dual-LLM screening with disagreement arbitration achieved 91% automation at 8% error, the best configuration tested in the closest operational analog (title/abstract screening). CheckEval-style boolean-checklist decomposition: +0.45 inter-evaluator agreement, +0.10 human correlation, and a 6-8-item checklist natively yields a graded 0-8 score from binary probes.

**Fit.** The only Anthropic-native path to an absolute P (no logprobs needed). N=10 votes give 0.1 granularity around the 2/3 threshold; ~$2-4/run. The correlated-error caveat is first-order for us: all-Haiku votes share Haiku's halo biases, so cross-family pooling (Haiku + gpt-4o-mini) and prompt-variant diversity are load-bearing, and family disagreement itself is a free abstention signal.

### 2.6 Local rerankers, embeddings, fine-tuning, learned combiners — mostly negative

**Citations.** FollowIR arXiv:2403.15246; mxbai/bge/jina/monoT5 (vendor cards); SPECTER2 arXiv:2211.13308; "A Little Bit Is Worse Than None" (SustaiNLP 2020); LTR small-query-set literature.

**Findings, mostly load-bearing negatives.** (i) Zero-shot relevance cross-encoders are trained on the axis our band has already saturated; FollowIR measured that non-instruction-tuned rerankers *ignore* stated non-relevance criteria (generally negative p-MRR). Honest expectation on our band: near-zero signal. Worth one free CPU afternoon as a baseline feature column only. (ii) Fine-tuning a small cross-encoder on our ~250 judged pairs has direct negative precedent — small in-domain fine-tuning can underperform the zero-shot model; no published success at 250 pairs. Lottery ticket at best. (iii) SPECTER2/SciNCL citation-proximity embeddings are still dominantly topical; given the lexical ranker's top-50 density is flat, a null result is the base case — use cosine(repo-wants, paper) as one feature, not a ranker. (iv) With 22 queries, tree-ensemble LTR will memorize repo idiosyncrasies; the defensible learner is L2 logistic regression over ≤ 6-8 features with leave-one-repo-out (LORO) CV, which natively outputs P(actionable) and respects the no-per-repo-knobs constraint by construction (global weights only).

### 2.7 Domain-side signals and gate restructuring

**Citations.** paper2repo arXiv:2004.06059 (prior-art scan); Lo/Nagappan/Zimmermann FSE 2015; adoption-vs-citation arXiv:2606.28120; HyDE arXiv:2212.10496; MMR (Carbonell & Goldstein 1998); McMaster PLUS; TRL-from-text (JASIST 2008).

**Findings.** (i) **No prior art exists** for repo-as-query → papers-ranked-by-actionability; the field runs the opposite direction (paper → its implementation repo). We are licensed to treat this as a novel utility-judgment task. (ii) **Citation-flavored features are dead on arrival**: the one dataset measuring practitioner relevance (512 Microsoft engineers × 571 papers) found *zero* correlation with citation counts, and adoption is decoupled from citation (Spearman 0.05-0.36). Its "unwise" taxonomy (unneeded tool, non-actionable study, doesn't generalize, cost > benefit) is a free rubric upgrade. (iii) **Implementability features are cheap and plausible**: has-released-code / repo stars / months-since-release via HF paper pages (complete-release repos: median 196 stars vs far lower otherwise) proxy the "someone could act on it" half of actionability — but not the fit-to-this-repo half, and recency compresses the range exactly where we operate. (iv) **Needs-first restructuring** (HyDE-shaped, genuinely untried): one derived-not-fitted call enumerates the repo's concrete open problems; the gate then asks *which enumerated need* a paper serves plus a separate maturity axis (McMaster's two-axis, wider-scale design; TRL work shows implementability is a recognizable textual register). Attacks the register mismatch — a repo describes what it HAS, useful papers describe what it should ADOPT — which is plausibly the root cause of diffusion-vs-vectordb indistinguishability. (v) **MMR near-duplicate collapsing** is expected-value-positive under the asymmetric −2 penalty (two papers on one inapplicable technique double the downside) with one global lambda; but with a flat relevance term it degenerates to farthest-point selection, so it composes with a better within-band score rather than replacing it. (vi) Judge-bias prompt fixes are near-free and measured: evidence-first rubric naming the specific repo component and concrete change before any score (+7.3 to +11.5pp for Claude judges), reference-guided grounding (math-judging failures 14/20 → 3/20).

## 4. Ranked experiment shortlist

All experiments run on the frozen Testbeds from the internal grounding — zero new judge calls. Primary: **Testbed A** = `results\judge-gpt-5.5-20260807T180938Z.json` (22 cases, 220 shown papers, 105 within-band, band membership reconstructed positionally from the sweep counts per the stable-sort argument; abstracts joined from `.work\full_pool\{case}.jsonl`, repo profiles from `.work\{case}`). Replication: the pool-300 arm (164310Z + the four db/storage single-case re-run files). Secondary: **Testbed B** = `diag_triage_prose300.json` (125-paper admitted band, 12 repos; target judge==3 ordering only, due to top-stratum saturation at judge ≥ 2). Sanity: **Testbed C** pooled (33 + 66 judged admits). Baselines fixed in advance: show-all +1.91 mean net@2, score-3-only +0.50, oracle = per-case act10; per-case sd 1.67, so all headline claims use paired per-case deltas plus a sign test.

### Experiment 1 — Shuffled subset selection with vote-share threshold (run this first)

**Hypothesis.** A single comparative selection call over the whole admitted set, repeated over shuffles, separates diffusion-like from vectordb-like bands where pointwise scores cannot, and the "possibly none" option abstains on all-bad bands.

**Procedure.** For each of the 17 pool-50 cases with a non-empty admit set: one Sonnet prompt containing the prose-300 repo profile plus all admitted papers (title+abstract, randomized ID labels), instruction: "select only the papers this repo's maintainers should actually act on — possibly none," with the evidence-first rubric (name the repo component and the concrete Monday-morning change before selecting). R = 15 shuffles of paper order at temperature 1. Score = selection share ∈ {0/15 … 15/15}. Pre-registered policy: digest = gate-3 papers + band papers with share ≥ 2/3. Haiku arm as cost ablation. Calls: 22 × 15 = 330, ~5-8k tokens each.

**Evaluated against.** Testbed A stored judge scores: (i) pooled within-band ROC-AUC of share vs judge ≥ 2, precision@k, cumulative net@2@k; (ii) headline policy net@2 vs +1.91; (iii) replication on the pool-300 arm; (iv) Testbed B: share-ordering scored on judge==3 density@10 vs reference points 2.4 / 3.1 / 5.3; (v) Testbed C pooled AUC.

**Success criterion (in advance).** Pooled within-band AUC ≥ 0.65 AND policy mean net@2 > +1.91 with ≥ 14/22 cases at delta ≥ 0, AND diffusion's full +10 retained (all 10 band papers clear 2/3), AND net improvement on at least two of {numerics −2, compiler −5, vectordb −5, linter −2}.

**Cost.** ~$3-6 (Sonnet), ~$0.50 (Haiku). **Kill condition.** Shares saturate (pooled mean > 0.8 with sd < 0.1); empty selections never occur on the linter/compiler bands across 15 shuffles; or AUC ≤ 0.55.

### Experiment 2 — Fine-scale distributional pointwise via OpenAI logprobs

**Hypothesis.** The score-2 pile-up is a quantization artifact; an 11-point anchored scale read as a logprob expectation, plus a reason-then-true/false p(true), recovers a usable continuous ordering and a thresholdable pseudo-P at near-zero cost.

**Procedure.** gpt-4o-mini with logprobs. Per paper (repo profile + title/abstract), two variants: (a) anchored 0-10 actionability score, anchors written from the existing rubric text (not from benchmark failures); score = Σ p(sᵢ)·sᵢ over the token distribution; (b) brief forced reasoning, then "true/false: maintainers should act on this"; P = normalized p(true). ~220 calls (Testbed A) + 125 (B) + 99 (C) ≈ 450 calls.

**Evaluated against.** Same Testbed A metrics as E1; calibration of p(true): Brier + reliability curve on all 220 shown papers; policy net@2 at pre-registered global P ≥ 2/3.

**Success criterion.** Pooled within-band AUC ≥ 0.65; promotion to calibration candidate iff Brier ≤ 0.22 and policy net@2 > +1.91.

**Cost.** <$1. **Kill condition.** Modal score token carries p > 0.9 for > 80% of papers (distribution degenerate, same collapse in finer clothes); AUC ≤ 0.55. **Mandatory validity check.** Judge is GPT-5.5 (same family): any win must survive intersection with the 200-row `second_judge.json` Sonnet verdicts before being believed.

### Experiment 3 — Ensemble vote fraction + verbalized P with the 2/3 rule (Anthropic-native calibration)

**Hypothesis.** Simulated-Annotators-style agreement over varied prompts yields an approximately calibrated P(actionable) that the utility-derived 2/3 threshold converts directly into the digest policy.

**Procedure.** Per band paper: N = 10 Haiku calls at temperature 1, each with a different rubric persona / few-shot set; each call must first state the strongest reason the paper is NOT actionable for this repo, then output verdict (act / not) + verbalized P (0-100). P̂ = mean of vote indicator and verbalized-P/100 (Avg-Conf). Optional panel arm: average with E2's p(true) (two families; disagreement logged as an uncertainty signal). Pre-registered policy: show iff P̂ > 2/3. ~2,200 Haiku calls (A) + 1,250 (B).

**Evaluated against.** Brier / log-loss / reliability curve on the 220 shown papers (the direct test of goal b); within-band AUC; policy net@2; LORO isotonic map as a diagnostic only (does the raw 2/3 rule's realized risk match nominal?).

**Success criterion.** Pooled ECE ≤ 0.15 and policy net@2 > +1.91 with the raw (un-recalibrated) 2/3 threshold; linter's band paper falls below threshold.

**Cost.** ~$2-4. **Kill condition.** P̂ clusters in 0.7-0.9 for nearly all papers regardless of judge label (the cross-family confident-wrong consensus pattern); ECE > 0.3; ordering AUC ≤ 0.55.

### Experiment 4 — Round-robin pairwise + Bradley-Terry, with template anchors (ordering backbone / fallback)

**Hypothesis.** Comparative utility judgments carry the within-band signal that absolute scores discard (the literature's largest measured deltas); anchors give the ordering an absolute zero for abstention.

**Procedure.** Per case: all pairs of admitted papers, both orders, Haiku, evidence-first comparative prompt; inconsistent pairs = ties; BT by MLE. Anchored arm: add 3 fixed-template anchor abstracts instantiated from the repo profile (clearly-actionable pattern / borderline / topically-adjacent-not-actionable); pre-registered policy: show iff P_BT(beats borderline anchor) ≥ 2/3. ~1,200 candidate pairs + ~600 anchor comparisons ≈ 1,800-2,500 calls.

**Evaluated against.** Testbed A within-band Kendall tau and AUC (primary — this experiment's job is ordering); Testbed B judge==3 ordering; anchored policy net@2 (secondary).

**Success criterion.** Pooled within-band AUC ≥ 0.70 (should beat pointwise per PRP/PairS if the mechanism transfers to utility) — that alone earns it the ordering-backbone role even if anchoring fails. Anchored policy graduates only if net@2 > +1.91.

**Cost.** ~$1-3. **Kill condition.** Swap-inconsistency > 45% of pairs (position bias drowning signal — a real risk given Claude-family measurements); anchor win-rates swing wildly under paraphrase of the anchor template (scale is fragile, calibration claim dead).

### Experiment 5 — Free-feature logistic chassis with LORO (cheapest calibration; also the ablation harness)

**Hypothesis.** Repo-independent metadata (has-code, stars, S2 influentialCitationCount, months-since-release, hop-coupling degree, HyDE rank, SPECTER2 cosine to repo wants) carries some within-band signal, and an L2 logistic regression over ≤ 8 features — including E1-E4 scores as columns — is the honest path to a pooled P(actionable).

**Procedure.** Assemble the feature matrix for the 220 Testbed A papers ($0: HF/S2 lookups, CPU embeddings, existing `.work\hyde_topk` and `.work\hop_pool` structural features); fit L2 logistic with LORO across the 22 repos (fit on 21, predict held-out, pool the 22 held-out sets); regularization strength by nested CV, never by benchmark score.

**Evaluated against.** LORO-pooled AUC and Brier vs judge ≥ 2; incremental AUC of each feature over the best single method from E1-E4.

**Success criterion.** Features-only LORO AUC ≥ 0.60 (else metadata is confirmed dead, a useful negative); combined model must beat the best single method's Brier to justify existing in the pipeline.

**Cost.** ~$0. **Kill condition.** Features-only AUC ≈ 0.5 — the expected outcome per FSE 2015 for citation-flavored features and the coupling/HyDE features' derivation-on-these-labels caveat (pitfall 7); in that case E1-E4's raw scores ship un-combined.

## 5. The calibration-vs-ordering distinction

These are different products and the metric treats them differently. **Pure ordering cannot rescue all-bad sets**: a perfect ranking of linter's band (precision 0.00) still shows papers at −2 each; permutation-style listwise and un-anchored pairwise BT are structurally forced to nominate a "best" paper even when the correct digest is empty. Ordering also cannot choose k: under net@2 each shown paper is worth 3p−2, so on an unranked or uniformly-scored set the optimal policy is show-everything (if p ≥ 2/3) or show-nothing — there is no interior optimum, and reordering a fully-shown top-10 cannot change net@2@10 at all (pitfall 8; every experiment must therefore report ordering metrics — AUC, precision@k — separately from policy value). **Only a calibrated P(actionable) with the global 2/3 threshold handles both tails simultaneously**: diffusion's band (true p = 1.00) all clears the threshold and keeps its +10; linter's band clears nothing and the case abstains at 0 instead of −2; and the +10/+4/+3 cases the score-3-only policy throws away are recovered because calibrated P, unlike the score-3 proxy, does not over-abstain. Mapping the shortlist: **E3** targets calibration directly (vote fraction + verbalized P is the construction with the best published ECE numbers); **E2's** p(true) is a cheap pseudo-P that needs a global recalibration check before its 2/3 threshold is trusted; **E1** delivers ordering plus *approximate* abstention — its selection share is peer-relative, so the linter test hinges entirely on whether "possibly none" fires, which is exactly what its kill condition probes; **E4** is ordering-first, gaining an absolute zero only through the unvalidated anchor mechanism; **E5** is calibrated by construction (logistic output) but only as good as its features. The pragmatic composite, if no single method wins both: E4-or-E1 ordering with E3-or-E2 supplying the absolute gate.

## 6. Guardrails

- **No per-repo knobs, all thresholds global.** The show/abstain threshold is 2/3, derived from net@2's payoff structure (threshold = FP-cost/(FP-cost+TP-gain) = 2/(2+1)), not from the benchmark. R (shuffles), N (votes), anchor templates, checklist items, and MMR lambda are single global constants fixed before any benchmark score is seen.
- **Pre-register one configuration per experiment.** The success criteria above are the pre-registration. No prompt iteration against the 22-case benchmark; if any fitting occurs (isotonic/Platt/logistic weights), it is LORO cross-fitted — fit on 21 repos, predict the held-out repo, pool — and the fitted map is global and frozen.
- **Offline-first.** Testbeds A/B/C cost zero judge calls; only ranker inference is spent. New judge labels are needed only if a policy admits papers outside the labeled shown/sampled sets — e.g., re-ranking full admitted pools at pool scale (12% label coverage, top-stratum-biased; the honest version costs ~$1.15 gate + ~$18 judge and is explicitly out of scope for this round). Keep all candidate selection inside the frozen labeled sets.
- **Label leakage.** Judge justifications and `proposed_change` sit beside the labels in `cache\judge\v1` and in the results files; no ranker prompt or feature may touch them. The coupling≥3 / HyDE-rank features and the r = +0.30 finding were derived on these same labels — anything built on them is LORO-only and reported as diagnostic.
- **Judge blindness.** The judge cache is keyed by (case, paper, rubric-hash), so verdicts are frozen per paper and method-agnostic — no ranker can influence its own grading. Preserve this: never re-judge a paper with method-specific context.
- **Judge-relativity and family confounds.** All labels are GPT-5.5-relative (second judge: same ranking, ~one notch stricter). Relative comparisons (method vs show-all) are safe; absolute net@2 claims and any OpenAI-model winner (E2) must be spot-checked against the 200 dual-judged rows in `.work\second_judge.json` to rule out same-family bias inflation.
- **Noise floors before victory laps.** Bound gate run-to-run churn with `diag_triage_prose.json` vs `diag_triage_prose300.json` (two draws of the same arm) before attributing small deltas to a new method; with per-case sd 1.67 over 22 cases, report paired per-case deltas and a sign test, never bare means. Per-repo judge==3 metrics are noise (1-14 positives per case) — pool always.
- **Bias mitigations are mandatory plumbing, not options.** Both-order querying for every pairwise call (Claude-family swap consistency measured as low as 23.8%), shuffle-aggregation for every listwise call (zero-shot listwise collapses under random input order), evidence-first rubric naming the repo component and concrete change before any score (+7.3 to +11.5pp for Claude judges), and no venue/author metadata ever shown to any judge (authority and style bias are the dominant measured biases).

## Pitfalls

1. LABELS EXIST ONLY FOR SHOWN/SAMPLED PAPERS. The 1,291 cached verdicts are (a) papers that reached some historical top-10 or were baseline picks, (b) P5's 320 stratified draws, (c) gate_full_pool's 73. An offline eval of a reranker over FULL admitted sets at pool scale (~25 admits from ~227 candidates per repo) is NOT possible from disk: only 12% of pool papers carry labels, and they are precisely the previously-shown top stratum. Doing that eval honestly costs ~$1.15 Haiku to gate all 4,984 pool papers plus ~$18 of judge (~550 admits × $0.032, minus cache hits). What IS fully covered offline: the shown top-10 sets of two complete 22-case arms (~420 labelled shown-paper slots), the 125-paper shipped-config admitted band on 12 repos, and pooled wild checks (33+66 admits).
2. TOP-STRATUM SELECTION BIAS. Within-band precision at judge>=2 is ~0.93 in diag_triage_prose300 (cache papers were surfaced by rankers/baselines) vs 0.38-1.00 (mean ~0.75) on the live pool-50 bands and 0.73 wild. A ranker that separates well on Testbed B may be modelling the stratum; Testbed A (live-run bands) is the honest primary and Testbed C the distribution check. RESULTS.md itself flags "the gold set is a TOP STRATUM".
3. BAND MEMBERSHIP IN TESTBED A IS RECONSTRUCTED, NOT RECORDED. run_judge_eval.py's _returned() drops llm_score, so per-paper gate scores rest on the stable-sort argument (triage.py sorted(..., reverse=True) on (llm_score, score_total)). The reconstruction gives exact band COUNTS and positional membership; it cannot recover the numeric gate score of papers below the min>=1 count. If exact per-paper gate scores are needed as ranker features, re-derive with Haiku (~$0.05) or use the diag files.
4. GATE STOCHASTICITY. The Haiku gate is not deterministic: diag_triage_prose.json vs diag_triage_prose300.json are two runs of the same arm over the same 602 (case,id) pairs with differing scores — use them to bound run-to-run band churn before attributing small deltas to a new ranker. A band frozen from one run is one draw of the gate.
5. JUDGE==3 SPARSITY. Only 41 judge-3s inside the shipped score-2 band (12 repos) and 16 among P5's judged admits; per-case counts are 1-14. Any per-repo score-3-ordering metric is noise; pool across repos, as the ranking diagnostic itself warns ("66 admitted papers carry a judge label... nowhere near enough to fit a ranker on").
6. ALL LABELS ARE GPT-5.5-RELATIVE. P7: a second judge ranks identically but sits one notch stricter (~0.55 multiplier on absolute rates). Relative comparisons (ranker A vs B, vs show-all) are safe; absolute net@2 claims should be spot-checked against .work\second_judge.json (200 dual-judged rows).
7. LEAKAGE ROUTES. Judge justifications/proposed_change live next to the labels in the cache and in the results files — feeding any judge text to the ranker is label leakage. The strata features (coupling>=3, HyDE rank) and the r=+0.30 finding were derived ON these same labels; a ranker hand-built around them and then "validated" on the same 22 cases is the reward-hacking the hard constraint forbids. Pre-register the policy (P>=2/3 threshold, no per-repo knobs); if any fitting happens, use leave-one-repo-out across the 22 cases.
8. METRIC SUBTLETIES. Reordering within a fully-shown 10 cannot change net@2@10 — only selection/abstention policies move the headline metric; report ordering (precision@k, AUC) and policy value (net@2) separately or the eval will look null by construction. Empty-band cases (rag, cli, http, webdev, encryption in pool-50) never reach the ranker and stay at net 0. In the pool-300 arm use the db/storage single-case re-run files, not the 22-case file's zeroed rows. Expected-net arithmetic (3p-2, breakeven 2/3) assumes lam=2 in net_actionable_value — keep lam consistent with evals\metrics.py.
9. ABSTRACT GAPS. 1/220 pool-50 shown papers is missing from full_pool jsonl; triage_papers.json covers only 911/1,275 unique cache ids — a Testbed that walks the whole cache needs one free arXiv batch fetch (fetch_papers in diagnose_triage.py already does this with caching).
