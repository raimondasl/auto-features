NERdME — typed README-span extraction as a second anchor channel

Lands in: profiler.py alongside _extract_anchors (currently manifest-only: requirements / pyproject / package.json)

The only idea in all 15 papers aimed at the record's measured open wound. NR-39 established that anchors are the sole discriminating channel (20.5% / 14.9% / 6.7% across score-3 / actionable / below) — and that 12 of 25 benchmark repos have zero anchors because only Python/JS manifests are parsed. NR-36 already proved the demand: its $0 probe showed 24/25 repos gain anchors from source scanning (compiler 0→43, db 0→35), but the shipped supply was bad (−0.52 net@2). Typed verbatim-span NER over READMEs is a different supply into the same measured demand.

It threads every recorded negative: verbatim spans, not paraphrase (NR-12 measured verbatim +21 over LLM rewrite); feeds the anchor/query side, not the gate (P8/NR-14 showed stated wants are toxic there); and the NERdME dataset — 200 annotated READMEs, 10 entity types — is free validation data. It also answers the self-scan's own observed weakness: TF-IDF pulled pytest, repo, digest as query terms; entity typing is exactly the filter that discards those.

$0 probe · kill condition Replicate the NR-36 probe protocol: run extraction (prompt-based Haiku first) over the 25 benchmark READMEs; count anchors gained on the 12 zero-anchor repos and spurious-anchor rate against NERdME's labels. Then re-run NR-39's grounding-rate measurement with the enriched anchor set — the non-Python coverage number NR-39 demands. Only if both move: live Tier B (frozen pools invalid for profile changes; floor 1.04 net@2/case).

OpenScholar's datastore — HyDE over a corpus wider than arXiv

Lands in: hyde.py + a second index alongside the 3.1M-vector arXiv shards

Not the paper's pipeline — its reranker re-runs the E1–E5 sweep that pointwise logprob already won, and its self-feedback loop is the "name what you lack" mechanism measured dead four times. Dense HyDE retrieval over OpenScholar's 45M open-access papers is the one untried variant that uses the mechanism the repo has proven rather than the one it has refuted.

LitSearch as a model-free recall gauge for the binarized index

Lands in: evals/, a standing diagnostic beside calibrate_finescale.py

Framed narrowly, deliberately: not a net@2 claim — LitSearch queries are researcher questions, a different register from repo→paper, so it cannot validate HyDE hypothesis quality. What it gives the project is its first external, model-free recall instrument for the index path itself. The binary-quantized index has bit-identical encoder verification (Hamming 0/1024) but no recall-fidelity gauge: nothing today would notice if binarisation, column pruning, or one bad yearly shard silently cost 15 points of recall. 597 gold-labelled queries over recent ML/NLP arXiv — squarely inside the index's coverage — fix that in an afternoon.

One afternoon · then a regression gate Embed the 597 queries, report recall@5/@20 against gold; optionally price the quantization by comparing binarised-Hamming vs full-precision cosine on the same encoder. Freeze the number; wire as an exit-nonzero gate (>2pt drop) after every rr sync-index.

Small notes, no builds

Record the MCP tool-count ceiling (paper #2's one number)

The architecture-patterns paper contributes exactly one transferable, quantitative finding: tool-selection accuracy degrades below 90% between 10–15 tools for Haiku-class models. The shipped server has 5 tools — 2× headroom — and the verifier confirmed nothing in the record tracks this. Worth a one-line constraint on Roadmap item 2's remaining distribution work: stay under ~10 tools; prefer parameterized tools over new ones. Everything else in the paper describes patterns mcp_server.py already exhibits.

Contamination re-analysis at $0 (from LitLLMs)

LitLLMs' rolling-test-set concern is real in principle: gold targets are ≥11-month-old papers almost certainly in every judge's training data. The repo's paired-delta design blunts it (P7's strictness offset cancels), but a free check exists: stratify the already-cached judge verdicts by publication date vs judge cutoff and test whether actionability rate or GPT-5.5/Sonnet agreement shifts post-cutoff. A re-analysis of stored data, not a new protocol.

Theme headers over the digest (from Eliot; status corrected to novel)

Eliot's trend-tracking premise is dead here (NR-5: all gold targets old, w_recency: 0.0), but clustering the ~15 shown papers under labelled theme headers is a harmless presentation nicety — MiniLM vectors are already cached, composition unchanged by construction, so no net@2 toll. The verifier corrected the analyst here: nothing in trends.py partially implements this; it is simply novel and small. Only worth doing opportunistically, with a test asserting digest-set equality.

One derived probe from paper #1, if ever tempted

The single reading of "Topic Is Not Agenda" that touches the live constraint (ordering within gate admits): agenda-community membership as a band-ordering signal. Every graph-derived ordering feature so far landed near chance (NR-21: AUC 0.585 vs the 0.60 bar; finescale incumbent 0.841), so the bar is explicit — use OpenAlex Topic IDs as a free community proxy on the 602-paper labelled set, kill below AUC 0.65. No Leiden build under any circumstance: the S2 truncation wall and the 6-repos-with-no-bibliography coverage hole price it out.

Why the other 25 mechanisms are skips

Not one skip is a taste call. Each lands on one of five walls the ledger has already paid to map — the grouping below is the actual causal structure of the rejections.

Wall 1 — Register mismatch: repo text describes what it has, useful papers describe what it should adopt

RESEARCH.md §1, §3.0–§3.2, NR-7, P2: with morphology fully fixed, "lacks" queries rank targets worse than random — a different-target failure no retrieval space fixes
  • LitLLMs' keyword-extraction retrieval — the exact mechanism, refuted four independent times; HyDE is the shipped answer that routes around it.
  • Paper #1's LLM-expanded Boolean retrieval — presumes a human-stated agenda; the repo-derived substitute is the most-refuted idea in the project.
  • ForeCite's term mining as query vocabulary — better-canonicalized phrases aimed at the wrong target still miss.
  • Metadata Enrichment's semantic tag expansion — operates entirely inside the topical-similarity relation.
  • OpenNovelty's claim→query pipeline — gap-derived query generation, Roadmap 19's verdict: dead.

Wall 2 — Similarity is the wrong relation: "similar to what this repo does" ≠ "would improve this repo"

RETRIEVAL_DESIGN.md Designs 1 & 3: even citation-trained SPECTER2 proximity surfaces ancestry, not improvements
  • Paper #1's citation-community audit — would confirm, at large-build cost, the conclusion the project reached with its own instrument. Its value is a citation for DRAFT.md, not a build.
  • ISLE's topic modeling and knowledge graph — similarity-space constructs; NR-39 just blocked the relation extension on 18/25 repos.
  • SCIDOCA's citation prediction — predicts what a text should cite: the ancestry relation, purest form. (Verifier correction: the 21/48 bibliography-seeded hop is evals/-only, not shipped — what ships is Feature 8's starred-seed proximity. The skip stands either way.)
  • Concept→origin-paper lookup — foundational papers of the repo's stack are what it already knows.

Wall 3 — Already shipped, and the upgrade path already ablated at zero

NR-33/NR-35: two ranking recompositions reshuffled ~59% of the digest and moved net@2 by exactly +0.00 — the gate and finescale downstream decide quality

  • LitLLMs' rerank-with-attribution — shipped stronger: E1–E5 tested the listwise/pairwise/ensemble family and pointwise logprob won (AUC 0.841).
  • OpenScholar's cross-encoder reranker — the roadmap entry (item 7) predates E1–E5 and should be re-derived against it.
  • Paper #1's citation-count rerank — w_citations exists, opt-in; the signal measured 1/24 as retrieval, AUC 0.585 as ordering.

Wall 4 — LLM enrichment of context measured strictly harmful

NR-12: verbatim beats paraphrase +21; full LLM paraphrase (+70) scores below sending nothing (+73); NR-13: structure-aware selection ≤ dumb 300-char prefix
  • MDKeyChunker's single-call enrichment — optimizes a cost the project never pays; the measured problem was the content, not the price. The paper's own ablation agrees: its no-LLM config beats its enriched pipeline (Recall@5 1.000 vs 0.867).
  • MDKeyChunker's structural chunking — NR-13's pre-registered showcase case scored +0 vs the prefix's +2; ceiling is what documents contain, not how they're read.

  • OpenScholar's self-feedback loop — multi-round refinement of a mechanism that aims wrongly; also needs exact logprobs Anthropic doesn't expose (NR-23).

The project's shipping bar: every stage carries a measured cost/worth line; C-14b: the MCP surface has zero benchmark coverage

  • Plan-then-generate review writing / cite-or-abstain synthesis — genuinely absent, correctly parked as Roadmap 15 (rr ask, v2.0): "a product bet, not a research one; judge on demand, not evidence." OpenScholar's recipe details keep for that day.

What the exercise says about the pipeline

The gate's 3/3 admits were the least buildable; the try-now idea sat in Maybe

Both direct admits — the embedding audit and the MCP patterns paper — produced zero build items, while NERdME, the one mechanism aimed at a measured open hole, never cleared the gate. The pattern is legible: the gate scores topical actionability, so papers about RepoRadar's own subject matter (paper discovery, MCP) score high, while instrument-shaped value — datasets, benchmarks, eval resources — reads as "not about this repo." That is a real, previously unrecorded observation about what the actionability rubric measures, and it echoes the record's own Tier S lesson: topical discrimination is not actionability.

Paper #1 is right, and acting on it would have cost +1.00 net@2

"Topic Is Not Agenda" concludes cosine neighborhoods encode topic, not agenda — RepoRadar's own central discovery, reached independently via Design 3. But its implied action (distrust w_embedding: 1.5) was resolved the other way by NR-38 days before this run: +1.00 net@2/case, sign p = 0.035, against a pre-registered negative prediction. The recorded lesson anticipates the trap exactly: retrieval-neighborhood statistics do not transfer to a tie-breaking role inside an already-concentrated pool. A true finding whose surface reading would have removed a measured gain — the sharpest argument in this whole exercise for keeping the ledger.

Two papers independently corroborate the ledger

MDKeyChunker's own ablation shows its no-LLM configuration beating its fully enriched pipeline — the repo's verbatim-beats-paraphrase finding (NR-12), reproduced by a paper that reached the digest without knowing it. And ISLE proposes, as its core contribution, the hybrid BM25+RRF fusion RepoRadar shipped a month earlier and has since ablated at +0.00. The literature is converging on conclusions the record already paid for; the digest is functioning partly as an external replication feed.

Method and limits. 14 agents: 4 ground readers distilled RESEARCH.md, ROADMAP.md, evals/RESULTS.md and paper/DRAFT.md plus a feature inventory of README and src/reporadar/; 8 analysts decomposed the papers (abstracts + gate reasons — full PDFs were not read); 2 adversarial verifiers re-checked all 37 status claims against the actual files, confirming 35 and correcting 2 (both corrections are reflected above). Worth levels are judgments against the project's recorded bar, not measurements — every build item above still owes its own $0 probe before anyone pays for a Tier B run.
